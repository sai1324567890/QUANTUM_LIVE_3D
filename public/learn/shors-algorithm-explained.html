Shor's Algorithm Explained: Factoring and RSA

Shor's algorithm factors a large integer N in polynomial time. It reduces factoring to finding the period r of f(x) = aˣ mod N, and a quantum Fourier transform finds that period efficiently.

From the period, ordinary number theory gives the factors of N. This would break RSA, whose security rests on factoring being hard for classical computers.

Today's machines are far too small and noisy. Published estimates for breaking RSA-2048 have fallen from about 20 million noisy qubits (2019) to under one million (2025), which is still far beyond current hardware. That is why post-quantum cryptography is being deployed now.

Frequently asked questions

Can Shor's algorithm break encryption today?

No. No existing quantum computer can run it at a useful size.

What replaces RSA?

Post-quantum algorithms such as the NIST standards ML-KEM and ML-DSA, which run on classical computers.
