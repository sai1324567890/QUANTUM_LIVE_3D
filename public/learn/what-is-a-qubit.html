What Is a Qubit? Bloch Sphere Explained

A qubit is a two-level quantum system, such as the spin of an electron, the polarisation of a photon or a tiny superconducting circuit. Its state is written |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ with |α|² + |β|² = 1.

Every pure qubit state is a point on the Bloch sphere. The north and south poles are |0⟩ and |1⟩. The angle θ sets the probabilities and the angle φ sets the relative phase.

Measuring a qubit gives 0 with probability |α|² and 1 with probability |β|². After the measurement the state collapses to the result you saw.

|ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^{iφ} sin(θ/2)|1⟩

Frequently asked questions

Is a qubit both 0 and 1 at the same time?

It is in a superposition, which is a definite quantum state with probabilities for each outcome. It is not simply a hidden 0 or 1.

What physical systems make qubits?

Superconducting circuits, trapped ions, neutral atoms, photons and electron spins are common choices.
