Quantum Tunneling Explained With a 3D Wave

In quantum mechanics a particle is a wave. When it meets a potential barrier higher than its energy, the wave does not stop sharply. Inside the barrier its amplitude decays exponentially.

If the barrier is thin, some amplitude comes out the other side, so the particle can be found beyond the barrier. The transmission probability falls very quickly as the barrier gets wider or taller.

Tunnelling explains alpha decay, nuclear fusion in stars, flash memory and the scanning tunnelling microscope, and it is the basis of some superconducting qubits.

Frequently asked questions

Does the particle dig through the barrier?

No. The wave extends into the barrier, and a measurement beyond it can succeed with a certain probability.

Where is tunnelling used in technology?

Flash memory, tunnel diodes, scanning tunnelling microscopes and Josephson-junction qubits.
