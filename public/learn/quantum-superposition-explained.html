Quantum Superposition Explained Simply

Superposition means a quantum system can be described by a sum of possible states, each with an amplitude. The squared size of an amplitude gives the probability of that outcome.

The double-slit experiment shows it clearly. A single electron passes through both slits as a wave, and the two paths interfere. The detected spots build up interference fringes even when particles are sent one at a time.

Superposition is fragile. Interaction with the environment (decoherence) turns it into ordinary uncertainty, which is why quantum computers need isolation and error correction.

Frequently asked questions

Is Schrödinger's cat really dead and alive?

It is a thought experiment showing how strange superposition would look at large scales. Real large objects decohere extremely fast.

How is superposition used in computing?

Algorithms prepare superpositions, let amplitudes interfere, then measure to read an answer.
