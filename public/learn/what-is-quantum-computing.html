What Is Quantum Computing? A Beginner's Guide

Quantum computing is a way of processing information using the rules of quantum mechanics. Instead of bits that are 0 or 1, it uses qubits, which can be in a combination of both states until measured.

A quantum computer does not simply try every answer at once. Algorithms are designed so that the amplitudes of wrong answers cancel out (destructive interference) and the amplitude of the right answer grows (constructive interference).

Quantum computers are expected to help with simulating molecules and materials, certain optimisation problems and, in the long run, breaking some current encryption. They will not replace everyday computers.

Frequently asked questions

Will quantum computers replace classical computers?

No. They are specialised machines for particular problems.

How many qubits do useful machines need?

It depends on the task. Fault-tolerant applications such as large-scale chemistry or factoring are estimated to need thousands of error-corrected logical qubits.
