Quantum Entanglement Explained: The Bell Test

Entangled particles share a joint state that cannot be written as separate states for each particle. Measuring one gives a random result, yet the results are strongly correlated with the other.

Bell tests check whether any local hidden-variable theory could explain such correlations. The CHSH quantity S is at most 2 for local theories but reaches 2√2 ≈ 2.83 in quantum mechanics. Experiments confirm the quantum value.

Entanglement powers quantum teleportation, entanglement-based key distribution and many quantum algorithms. It cannot be used to send a message faster than light.

S = |E(a,b) − E(a,b′) + E(a′,b) + E(a′,b′)| ≤ 2√2

Frequently asked questions

Does entanglement allow faster-than-light communication?

No. Each side sees random results; the correlation only shows up when the two records are compared.

Who won the 2022 Nobel Prize for Bell tests?

Alain Aspect, John Clauser and Anton Zeilinger, for experiments with entangled photons that established the violation of Bell inequalities.
